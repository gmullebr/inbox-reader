Inbox Reader privacy policy

Last changed on 25 October 2026

Inbox Reader keeps a person's email on that person's own devices. It sends Google user data to no one, and no person other than the user reads it.

1Data the app reads

At sign-in, the user grants Inbox Reader Google's permission to read Gmail and change its labels. With that permission, the app reads the threads in the user's inbox: the sender, subject, date and labels of each message, and its text. Google's sign-in also gives the app the user's name and email address.

2How the app uses it

The app shows the inbox on the user's own device and tags each thread with fixed rules that run on the device. It changes Gmail in three ways, each at the user's request. Archiving removes the Inbox label from a thread, undo adds it back, and opening a thread removes the Unread label. The app never deletes, sends, replies to or forwards a message.

3Where the data stays

The inbox list and the message text stay in the app's storage on the user's device, and the sign-in tokens stay in the device's keychain. The app removes a thread's content from the device once the thread leaves the inbox. Signing out removes all of it. One item leaves the device: a tag the user sets by hand, stored as Gmail's thread and message identifiers with no content, which syncs between the user's own devices through the user's own iCloud account.

4Sharing

The app has no server, no analytics and no advertising, and it shares Google user data with no third party. Its other features make network requests that carry no personal data. Inbox Reader's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

5Ending access

Signing out in the app's settings deletes the data the app keeps on that device. Removing Inbox Reader under the third-party connections of the Google account, at myaccount.google.com/connections, ends its access at Google.

6Changes

The date at the top of this page shows the last change. The household member who set up the app answers questions about it.
